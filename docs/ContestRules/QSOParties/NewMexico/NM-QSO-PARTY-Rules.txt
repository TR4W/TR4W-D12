                             2026 New Mexico QSO Party Rules

1. OBJECT:
         - New Mexico stations: Work anyone for NM counties, states, provinces, and DX.
         - Non-NM stations: Work NM stations for NM counties only.

2. CONTEST PERIOD:
         - Second Saturday of April, 8:00 MDT (1400 UTC) to 20:00 MDT (0200 UTC).
         - Duration: 12 hours (full-time operation permitted).

3. CONTEST BANDS AND SUGGESTED FREQUENCIES:
         - Contest bands: 160-10 meters, plus 6 and 2 meters
                    i. Not permitted: WARC bands, 60 meters, and bands above 2 meters
         - Suggested frequencies (on or near):
                    i. CW: 1820, 3550, 7045, 14050, 21050, 28050 kHz, 50.095 MHz, 144.09 MHz
                    ii. Phone: 1850, 3815, 7260, 14280, 21380, 28380 kHz, 50.130 MHz, 144.20 MHz
                        SSB, 146.52 MHz FM
                   iii. Digital: Around each mode's customary calling frequencies
                             1. Attention FT8/FT4 users: For an FT8/FT4 QSO to be valid, it must
                                  include the required QSO exchange from both stations (see Section 6)
                                  and be logged in the same format as QSOs in other modes. QSOs that
                                  contain grid squares or signal reports instead of the required exchange
                                  cannot be scored by the New Mexico QSO Party's automated scoring
                                  software. Participants using FT8/FT4 must either use a mode that
                                  supports the required exchange (such as JS8Call) or manually edit their
                                  logs prior to submission by replacing signal reports with "599" and
                                  converting grid squares to the appropriate state, province, or DXCC
                                  entity.
                   iv. New Mexico stations are encouraged to operate on/near these frequencies,
                        especially at the top of each hour.

4. CONTEST MODES:
         - Phone, CW, digital (any computer-to-computer mode is considered digital)

5. CLASSES:
         - Single Operator. One person performs all operating and logging functions. Only one
              (1) transmitted signal on the air at any time. All transmitters, receivers, and antennas
              must lie within a 500-meter diameter circle. This includes portable (non-mobile)
              stations.

         - Multi-Operator (New Mexico stations only). More than one person performs operating
              or logging functions. Only one signal per band per mode allowed at any time. All
              transmitters, receivers, and antennas must lie within a 500-meter diameter circle. This
              includes portable (non-mobile) stations.

         - Mobile. Consistent with County Hunter rules
              (https://www.marac.org/documents/award-rules.pdf), "making contacts using a radio in
              or on a movable vehicle is a Mobile Operation. The vehicle does not have to be in
              motion. The radio must be powered by the vehicle or other power source that is
              contained in the vehicle and which is independent of all commercial or other outside
              power sources. The vehicle must be capable of movement without having to
              disconnect any wiring or antennas in order to move to another location. Examples of
              vehicles that are capable of mobile operation include automobiles, trucks, RVs, trains,
              bicycles, airplanes, and buses." All operations occur within the same vehicle. There are
              two classes of mobile operation:
                                                                                                                                Page 1 of 6

                             2026 New Mexico QSO Party Rules

                    i. Single Operator. One person performs all operating, logging, and driving
                        functions. For safety reasons, participants should operate only when vehicle is
                        not in motion.

                    ii. Multi-Operator. More than one person performs operating, logging, and driving
                        functions. For safety reasons, participants should operate only while there is a
                        dedicated driver or while vehicle is not in motion.

6. EXCHANGE:
         - New Mexico stations: RS(T) + New Mexico county
         - Non-New Mexico stations: RS(T) + State/Province/DXCC entity

7. QSO POINTS:
         - Each Phone contact = 1 point
         - Each CW contact = 2 points
         - Each Digital contact = 2 points

8. MULTIPLIERS AND BONUSES:
         - Power multiplier based on maximum power used:
                    i. QRP (5 watts or less): x5
                    ii. Low Power (> 5 - 150 watts): x2
                   iii. High Power: (> 150 watts): x1

         - Location Multipliers for New Mexico participants
                    i. Multiply QSO points by total of each different NM county worked (up to 33),
                        states worked (up to 50), Canadian provinces/territories worked (up to 13; see
                        below) and DX entities worked.
                             1. Alaska and Hawaii count as state multipliers only; District of Columbia
                                  (DC) counts as Maryland.
                             2. United States and Canada do not count as DX entity multipliers.
                             3. Each multiplier may be counted ONLY ONCE, regardless of mode or
                                  band.
                    ii. List of Canadian Provinces and Territories: Alberta (AB), British Columbia (BC),
                        Manitoba (MB), New Brunswick (NB), Newfoundland-Labrador (NL), Nova
                        Scotia (NS), Ontario (ON), Prince-Edward Island (PE), Quebec (QC),
                        Saskatchewan (SK), Northwest Territories (NT), Nunavut (NU), Yukon
                        Territories (YT)

         - Location Multipliers for Non-New Mexico Participants
                    i. Multiply QSO points by total of each different NM county worked (up to 33).
                    ii. Each multiplier may be counted ONLY ONCE regardless of mode or band.

         - Mobile Operations Bonus (for New Mexico mobile stations only)
                    i. Add an additional 5,000 points to the final score for every county from which at
                        least 15 valid QSOs were made.

         - W1AW/5 Bonus (new, for 2026 only)
                    i. Add an additional 250 points to your final score for a valid contact with
                        W1AW/5. Bonus points are valid for one (1) W1AW/5 QSO only, regardless of
                        band, mode, or W1AW/5's county.
                             1. Participants logging electronically: Enter a clear note in the Cabrillo
                                  "soapbox" field to indicate that you worked W1AW/5 to claim this credit.
                                  We will verify the QSO within your log data.

                                                                                                                                Page 2 of 6

                             2026 New Mexico QSO Party Rules

                    ii. W1AW/5 may be on the air from multiple New Mexico counties. Stations may
                        work W1AW/5 again on the same band and mode, only if W1AW/5 is operating
                        from a different county than previous contacts. Each county activation is
                        considered a new station for QSO point credit; however, the W1AW/5 bonus
                        may be claimed only once.

                   iii. W1AW/5 stations will include their New Mexico county as part of the standard
                        exchange.
                             · NOTE: W1AW/5 stations will not be competing for standings or awards
                                  in the New Mexico QSO Party. They will only submit a check-log for
                                  QSO verification purposes.

9. SCORING:
         - For non-mobile participants:
              Total = (Sum of QSO points from all bands x power multiplier x location multiplier) +
              W1AW/5 contact bonus

         - For mobile participants:
              Total = (Sum of QSO points from all bands and counties operated from x power
              multiplier x running total of location multipliers) + Mobile operation bonus + W1AW/5
              contact bonus
                        (For example, if you work GA, NC, and BER from one county, and BER, IN, OH
                        from the next county you operate from, your running total of location multipliers
                        is five [5]. BER may only be counted as a multiplier once.)

10. MISCELLANEOUS:
         - Stations may be worked only once per mode, per band.
                    i. For 2026 only, W1AW/5 may be worked multiple times on the same band and
                        mode when operating from different New Mexico counties (see Section 8).
                        Each county is considered a new station for QSO point credit.
         - Complete exchange information must be received by each station for a complete QSO.
         - All contacts must be made without the assistance of intermediary stations, such as
              repeaters, digipeaters, satellites, etc.
         - Spotting and self-spotting is permitted.
         - County lines are defined as per County Hunter rules
             (https://www.marac.org/documents/award-rules.pdf).
         - A New Mexico mobile station that travels into a new county is considered to be a new
              station and may be contacted again for point and multiplier credit. A New Mexico
              mobile station that travels into a new county may contact other stations again for point
              credit (however multipliers may still only be counted once).
         - Consistent with County Hunter rules (https://www.marac.org/documents/award-
              rules.pdf) "a stationary mobile may operate on a County Line and contacts with the
              mobile can be used as credit for both counties." For reasons of safety and access,
              contacts made within 50 meters of a county line are considered valid for county line
              operations. Furthermore, "only one County Line at a time can be run for [New Mexico
              QSO Party] credit. If the mobile is operating from the intersection of three or more USA
              Counties, only two counties at a time may be counted for the same contact."

11. SPECIAL CATEGORIES AND AWARDS:
         - High Score - New Mexico Club award. A plaque, if sponsored, will be awarded to the
              New Mexico amateur radio club with the highest aggregate score, based on the scored
              logs submitted by its participating members. To receive credit for this award, the club
              name must be clearly identified in each participant's submitted log (paper or

    electronic). Each participant may list only one New Mexico club and must be a member
    in good standing of that club at the time of the QSO Party.
- Most Counties Activated by a New Mexico Club award. A plaque, if sponsored, will
    be awarded to the New Mexico amateur radio club with the highest aggregate number
    of counties activated, based on the counties activated by its participating members as
    recorded in their submitted logs. A minimum of 15 QSOs must be made from a county
    for it to count toward this award. To receive credit for this award, the club name must
    be clearly identified in each participant's submitted log (paper or electronic). Each
    participant may list only one New Mexico club and must be a member in good standing
    of that club at the time of the QSO Party.

- New Mexico Expedition Category: This special category is intended to encourage
    participants to activate counties using temporary, field-deployed stations that are set up
    specifically for the New Mexico QSO Party.
          i. Eligibility and Qualification: To qualify as an Expedition station, the operator
              must make at least 30 valid QSOs during the event. The station must be
              assembled and operated specifically for the New Mexico QSO Party and must
              not rely on a permanently installed station.
          ii. Definition of an Expedition Station: An Expedition station is a portable (non-
              mobile) station that is temporarily set up in the field for the purpose of
              participating in the contest. This category is distinct from Mobile operations, as
              all contacts are made from a fixed location rather than from within a moving or
              self-contained vehicle.
         iii. Operating Class and Scoring: The Expedition Category is a special
              designation and not a separate operating class. Participants must still enter as
              either Single Operator or Multi-Operator, as defined in Section 5. Expedition
              stations will be scored and ranked within their selected operating class, and will
              also be recognized separately among other Expedition participants.
         iv. Identification in Logs: Stations wishing to be recognized in the Expedition
              Category must clearly indicate this in their submitted logs. For electronic
              submissions, this should be included in the Cabrillo "soapbox" field. For paper
              logs, this should be clearly noted on the summary sheet.
          v. Station Requirements and Restrictions: Expedition stations must not use
              pre-existing stations, permanently installed antennas, or fixed radio facilities,
              whether amateur or commercial. All equipment must be temporarily deployed
              for the event. All equipment used for the Expedition must be powered by
              sources that are independent of commercial power mains. Acceptable power
              sources include batteries, generators, solar panels, fuel cells, wind generators,
              or similar self-contained systems. All transmitters, receivers, and antennas
              must be located within a 500-meter diameter circle, consistent with the
              requirements for Single Operator and Multi-Operator stations.
         vi. County Line Operations: Expedition stations may operate from a county line,
              and contacts may be credited for both counties. For safety and practical access
              considerations, stations located within 50 meters of a county boundary are
              considered valid for county line operation. If operating from a location where
              three or more counties meet, only two counties may be counted for any given
              contact. Only one county line may be activated at a time.
        vii. Relocation During the Event: An Expedition station may be disassembled,
              moved, and reassembled in a different county or at a different county line
              during the contest. In order for each location to qualify as a valid Expedition
              activation, the station must meet the minimum requirement of 30 QSOs at that
              location. When an Expedition station relocates to a new county, it is considered

                                                                                                                     Page 4 of 6

                             2026 New Mexico QSO Party Rules

                        a new station for contact purposes. Other stations may work it again for QSO
                        point credit. However, multipliers may still only be counted once, regardless of
                        relocation.
                 viii. Mobile Bonus Exclusion: Expedition stations are not eligible to receive Mobile
                        Operations Bonus points.
         - High Score - QRP award. A plaque, if sponsored, will be awarded to the highest-
              scoring New Mexico single-operator station (excluding mobile bonus points) operating
              at a power output of 5 watts or less for the entire contest.
         - High Score - YL/XYL award. A plaque, if sponsored, will be awarded to the highest-
              scoring female New Mexico single-operator station (excluding mobile bonus points).

12. REPORTING:
         - Cabrillo formatted electronic logs. A list of known logging applications that support
              New Mexico QSO Party rules is available on the New Mexico QSO party website at
              www.newmexicoqsoparty.org. Electronic logs must be submitted in proper Cabrillo
              format (template included below). Electronic logs that are incomplete, not in proper
              Cabrillo format, or late will be counted only as check logs.
         - Paper logs. Valid paper logs must include (a) a completed check sheet; (b) completed
              log sheets; and (c) a completed summary sheet. Only forms provided on the New
              Mexico QSO party website at www.newmexicoqsoparty.org will be accepted. Paper
              logs that are incomplete, illegible, or late will be counted only as check logs.
         - Participant logs must be received no later than April 25, 2026 (two weeks following
              the event).
         - Electronic logs should be emailed to logs@newmexicoqsoparty.org
         - Paper logs should be submitted via postal mail to:

                                  New Mexico QSO Party
                                  c/o Amateur Radio Caravan Club
                                  PO Box 36365
                                  Albuquerque, NM 87176-6365

         - NMQP will strive to post final results on the New Mexico QSO Party website in the
              June timeframe.

13. AWARDS AND PARTICIPANT CERTIFICATES:
         - Plaques will be awarded to first place winners of various categories. Winning Multi-Op
              teams will receive a single plaque on behalf of their team. If a plaque for a particular
              category does not have a sponsor by the time logs are scored, a color certificate will be
              awarded instead. A list of available awards and sponsors is provided at
              www.newmexicoqsoparty.org. Sponsorship of awards may be changed at the
              discretion of the New Mexico QSO Party organizers based on contest category entries.
         - Special awards may be made at the discretion of the New Mexico QSO Party
              organizers.
         - Participant certificates will be available online via the NMQP website for all stations
              who submit their electronic or paper log by the submission deadline. Certificates will
              become available when official results are posted to the NMQP website.

                      New Mexico QSO Party Cabrillo Log Format

Log developers: Please utilize the following template to support the New Mexico QSO Party:

                   --------INFO SENT-----          -------INFO RCVD------

QSO: FREQ MO DATE  TIME CALL             RST QTH   CALL      RST QTH

QSO: ***** ** YYYY-MM-DD NNNN ********   *** ****  ********  *** ****

QSO: 14000 PH 2010-02-07 1501 KB5SXB/M   59 BER    NK5W      59 SAN

- All QSO data should be space delimited for proper parsing.
- Cabrillo version 2.0 or 3.0 welcome.
- All dates/times are in UTC
- Modes are PH, CW, or RY. Use RY to designate all digital modes.
- QTH is three letter NM county abbreviation, or two-letter State/Province abbreviation, or "DX".
- The ARRL-SECTION: tag (Cabrillo v2.0) and LOCATION: tag (Cabrillo v3.0) is used to

    identify New Mexico stations for proper automated log checking and scoring. Cabrillo logs
    generated for New Mexico stations should reflect ARRL-SECTION: NM or LOCATION: NM.
    Cabrillo logs generated for non-NM stations should reflect their respective location (example:
    ARRL-SECTION: EPA, LOCATION: PA, LOCATION: DX etc.).
- Cabrillo log filenames are call.log, where "call" is the NMQP participant's callsign.
